# What else does this job today

Checked on 13 October 2026. "This job" means helping people understand how
climate change is affecting northern winter roads, so they can plan, travel and
make decisions.

## Short answer

No single tool does what WRaMP does. Today people piece the job together from
live-status maps, climate portals, government open data and community apps.
The project's 2025 NWT research licence states that no comprehensive map of
winter surface transportation exists in print or online, and nothing found in
this review contradicts that.

**What only WRaMP does.** It is the only tool found that combines current road
opening and closing history (through the 2025–26 season) with homogenized
station climate data, freezing degree days and plain-language trend
statistics.

**Where others are ahead:**

- live road status (DriveNWT and the road operators)
- climate projections (ClimateData.ca, Climate Atlas of Canada)
- bulk data downloads (Environment and Climate Change Canada)
- size of community, Indigenous-language support and community control of
  data (SIKU)

## What this means for WRaMP

1. **Don't build live road status.** Link each road to DriveNWT or to its
   operator.
2. **Show projections next to the historical trends.** Planners need both, and
   ClimateData.ca already provides projections.
3. **Publish the road dataset as CSV and through an API.** It's the one asset
   no one else has kept current; the public GNWT dataset stops in 2021. Check
   the data licence with GNWT first.
4. **Talk to SIKU and the guardian programs** about sharing observations,
   rather than growing Winter Road Watch alone.
5. **Keep the plain-language road statistics.** No other tool found offers
   them.
